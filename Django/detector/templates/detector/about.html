About This Project

Methodology

This tool compares several machine learning approaches for classifying banana leaf photos: classical models (SVM, Random Forest) trained on extracted image features, alongside deep learning models (a custom CNN, ResNet18, and MobileNetV3) trained directly on leaf images. The best-performing model, selected using a consistent evaluation script across all candidates, is the one running behind this site.

Predictions are paired with Grad-CAM visualisations that highlight which parts of the leaf most influenced the model's decision, to support interpretability rather than a black-box result.

Biosecurity Context

Black Sigatoka (Pseudocercospora fijiensis) is a fungal leaf disease not established on the Australian mainland, but present on outer Torres Strait islands and in every country to Australia's north. It was detected on an inner Torres Strait island as recently as 2023, and is under active surveillance by Northern Australia Quarantine Strategy (NAQS) plant pathologists based in Darwin.

It is a formally notifiable plant disease. A 2001 outbreak in north Queensland's main commercial banana-growing region triggered a multi-year eradication response, illustrating the cost of late detection.

Limitations

  • This tool is a first-pass screening aid, not a diagnostic substitute for a laboratory test or an expert plant pathologist.
  • Model accuracy depends on the training datasets used and may not generalise perfectly to every lighting condition, leaf angle, or growth stage.
  • Suspected cases should always be reported through official biosecurity channels, regardless of what this tool shows.
